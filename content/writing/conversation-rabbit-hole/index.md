## The rabbit holes

<section class="rabbit-grid">
  <article class="rabbit-card">
    <p class="rabbit-number">01</p>
    <h3>What is the “general will”?</h3>
    <p>Rousseau’s idea is not simply “whatever most people want.” It is the supposed common interest of the whole political community. That sounds elevating, but Acemoglu worries that someone must still decide what counts as that common interest.</p>
    <p><strong>The real disagreement:</strong> Can politics rest on a shared moral truth, or must free people keep negotiating temporary agreements without pretending the argument has been settled forever?</p>
    <p><a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rousseau/">Rousseau and the general will</a> · <a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberty-positive-negative/">Positive and negative liberty</a></p>
  </article>

  <article class="rabbit-card">
    <p class="rabbit-number">02</p>
    <h3>Noninterference is not nondomination</h3>
    <p>“Leave me alone” is noninterference. “No one has arbitrary power over me” is nondomination. A benevolent boss, landlord, spouse, or state can leave you alone today and still hold power that makes you unfree tomorrow.</p>
    <p><strong>Why it matters here:</strong> This gives Acemoglu a case for institutions that protect people from dependence without saying the state should run every part of life.</p>
    <p><a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/">A guide to republican freedom</a></p>
  </article>

  <article class="rabbit-card">
    <p class="rabbit-number">03</p>
    <h3>Automation versus new tasks</h3>
    <p>Acemoglu is not saying machines are bad. He separates technology that replaces a task from technology that gives people new tasks, skills, or complementary work. His concern is a world that gets very good at the first and neglects the second.</p>
    <p><strong>Keep this distinction:</strong> a falling <em>labor share</em> and falling <em>wages</em> are different claims. Cowen presses that difference throughout this exchange.</p>
    <p><a href="https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.33.2.3">Acemoglu and Restrepo on automation and new tasks</a> · <a href="https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/705716">Their robot-labor-market evidence</a></p>
  </article>

  <article class="rabbit-card">
    <p class="rabbit-number">04</p>
    <h3>Speech versus amplification</h3>
    <p>Acemoglu draws his line between your right to speak and a platform’s ability to learn your vulnerabilities, choose who sees your speech, and keep feeding people what inflames them. Cowen’s counterexample is the printing press: dangerous ideas can spread through older media too.</p>
    <p><strong>The question to carry:</strong> Is a recommendation system just a modern distributor, or a distinct kind of power because it personalizes persuasion at scale?</p>
    <p><a href="https://www.loc.gov/resource/afc2001001.42469/?sp=3">Frederick Douglass on free speech</a> · <a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freedom-speech/">Free-speech background</a></p>
  </article>

  <article class="rabbit-card">
    <p class="rabbit-number">05</p>
    <h3>Immigration is not the education argument</h3>
    <p>Acemoglu says he favors high-skilled immigration—including the H-1B route he used. His concern is different: whether leaders use immigration as an excuse to stop improving education and training for Americans already here.</p>
    <p><strong>The useful split:</strong> “Should skilled immigrants be admitted?” and “Who is responsible for building domestic capability?” can both be answered yes.</p>
    <p><a href="https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations">How H-1B works</a></p>
  </article>

  <article class="rabbit-card">
    <p class="rabbit-number">06</p>
    <h3>What “pro-worker AI” means</h3>
    <p>It is not a regulator approving every model. In Acemoglu’s definition, it is AI that expands what nurses, electricians, journalists, and other workers can do—rather than merely removing tasks and concentrating the gains elsewhere.</p>
    <p><strong>A practical test:</strong> after this tool arrives, does the person doing the work have more judgment, capability, and leverage—or simply less work to do until they are no longer needed?</p>
    <p><a href="https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487">The productivity paper Cowen asks him to revise</a> · <a href="https://www.nber.org/papers/w33363">Acemoglu, Autor, Beirne, and Scott on demographics and innovation</a></p>
  </article>
</section>
